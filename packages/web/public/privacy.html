Privacy

cuesea helps a retail associate help the person in front of them. This page says what that requires us to collect, what we deliberately refuse to collect, and which promises the software currently keeps.

Effective 14 August 2026 · Version 1.0

The short version

There is no camera on the glasses and no facial recognition anywhere in the product. A customer is only ever recognised if they opted in with the retailer and identified themselves — by scanning a code — at that visit. We store a reference to the retailer's own customer record, never a copy of it. The microphone opens only when an associate deliberately presses for it, and closes itself when they stop speaking.

Who is responsible for what

cuesea is sold to retailers. When a retailer uses it in their stores, the retailer decides what customer data exists and why; we process it on their instructions. In data-protection terms they are the controller and we are the processor. If you are a shopper and want to know what a particular retailer holds about you, or want it deleted, that request goes to them — and we support them in answering it.

For the accounts we create for store staff, and for our own service logs, we are the controller.

What the glasses do and don't capture

Display and input

The Even Realities G2 renders text and takes gestures. It has no camera and no speaker. The omission is deliberate, not an oversight of a first version: a camera on a shop floor changes what the product is, and what it would take to approve it.

Microphone

Our app requests the g2-microphone permission. It is not a continuous recording. The microphone opens only on an explicit double press by the associate, streams audio while they ask a question, and closes automatically when they stop speaking — with a hard limit of a few seconds. A live level indicator is shown in the lens the entire time it is open, so it is never ambiguous whether the microphone is live.

The audio is transcribed so the question can be answered, and the audio itself is not retained after that. The transcript is not stored either, unless a retailer explicitly turns transcript storage on for their own account; it is off by default. What we keep is the kind of question it was, whether it was answered, and how long it took.

The feature is designed for the associate to ask a question, not to capture a conversation. Retailers are responsible for how they use it, including under laws that require all parties to consent to a recording.

Network

Our app requests the network permission and talks to exactly one host — our own service at realtime-production-80f4.up.railway.app. No advertising networks, no third-party analytics in the glasses app, and no keys of any kind inside the installed package.

What we do not store

  • Customer profiles. Interactions carry the retailer's own identifier for a customer and nothing else — no name, no contact details, no purchase history copied across. Those stay in the retailer's system, and we read them only when needed to render a recommendation.
  • Images or video. There is no camera on the glasses.
  • Biometric identifiers. No face, voice, or gait recognition is used to identify anyone, at any point.
  • Location traces. We record which zone of a store an interaction happened in. We do not request or record device location.
  • Audio recordings. See above.

Who else is involved

We use a small number of service providers, each with a specific job:

  • Railway — runs our services and database (United States).
  • Vercel — serves our web interfaces.
  • Even Realities — makes the glasses and the phone app that carries our display to them.
  • The retailer's own commerce platform (for example Shopify) — the source of product and customer information, connected by the retailer with their own credentials.
  • A speech-to-text provider, where a retailer has enabled real transcription, to convert a spoken question to text. Audio is sent for that purpose only and is not used to train their models.

We do not sell personal information, and we do not share it for advertising.

How long we keep things

Each retailer has a retention setting on their account, which defaults to 90 days for interaction records.

Being straight about the current state: that setting is recorded and honoured in what we newly collect, but automatic deletion of older records is not yet running in the product. Until it is, deletion happens on request — a retailer can ask us to remove their data, or a specific customer's records, and we will do it. We would rather say this plainly than describe a schedule the software does not yet keep. This page will be updated when enforcement ships.

Staff accounts and their activity are kept for as long as the retailer uses cuesea, and removed when the account is closed.

Security

  • Traffic is encrypted in transit.
  • Passwords are stored as salted scrypt hashes; we never see or store the original.
  • Sign-in tokens are stored hashed, so a copy of our database does not hand over live sessions.
  • Every store's data is separated at the query level, through a single code path built for that purpose rather than a convention applied by hand.
  • Staff can only see their own store. The people who can see across retailers are cuesea employees, on a separate internal system that customers cannot reach.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to see what is held about you, correct it, delete it, or object to its use. If you are a shopper, contact the retailer you shopped with; they hold the relationship and we act on their instruction. If you are a member of store staff, or you would rather come to us directly, write to the address below and we will help.

Changes

If we change this page materially we will update the version and effective date above, and tell the retailers using cuesea. We won't quietly broaden what we collect.
