06 / Why us

Three claims survive scrutiny. Three is enough.

01

Zero-lookup delivery

Endear, Tulip, Salesfloor, Clientbook and Shopify POS all require the associate to break eye contact, pick up a device and search. cuesea is the only one where the answer arrives without leaving the interaction. The claim is about the moment, not the frame — it holds on a wrist, an earpiece, a handheld or a lens.

02

Grounded, not generated

Answers come from records held, never a model's guess about stock. “I can't see stock right now” is a different sentence from “we don't carry that”, and we treat that distinction as core engineering. 54% of retailers name integration and 40% name data quality as their top AI blocker — honesty about what we don't know is an enterprise claim, not a feature.

03

Consent as architecture

Camera-free by default. Opt-in at every tier — tap, QR, wallet pass — never passive matching. No ambient recording of customers. Per-tenant sealed credentials that no cuesea surface and no cuesea employee can read back. This is precisely what dies in an enterprise security review, and it is already built.

10 / Risk

The bear case, in our own words.

Everything below is the argument against us. We would rather hand it to you than have you find it.

No publishing path

Meta's wearables toolkit is developer preview only — its own FAQ says publishing is not available. No store, no permission model, no MDM.

No fleet procurement

Ray-Ban Display is US-only and international expansion was paused in January 2026 on supply. A forty-store chain cannot buy four hundred units.

Supply below the user base

~3.0M display glasses ship globally in 2026. The US alone employs 4.21M retail sales workers.

The incumbent's ceiling

Vuzix has sold head-worn computing to frontline workers for fifteen years. FY2025: $6.3M revenue, $32.3M net loss.

Retail voted mobile

Kroger's associate assistant is mobile. Shopify shipped a handheld in June 2026. Zero named retail sales-floor glasses deployments exist anywhere.

And the seed bar is revenue

Many 2026 funds want $300–500k ARR at seed. We are pre-revenue with an unsigned pilot — which is exactly why this is a pre-seed.

So we are not a glasses company. We are the context layer, delivered to whatever the associate already carries — phone, handheld, earpiece or lens. Glass is an option we own cheaply: demoed live, promised for nothing. Convergence plausibly lands 2027–2028, and we will already be there.

11 / The plan

Spend the money on exactly one number.

Incremental revenue per associate per shift.

Measured in three pilot chains, against a holdout. Tulip publishes a decade of ROI case studies — we cannot out-market that. We can out-measure it.

Months 0–3

Prove the answer

  • Completeness semantics shipped — “I can't see stock” separated from “we don't carry it”
  • Shopify App Store listing live; self-serve onboarding end to end
  • Three paid twelve-week pilots signed, holdout designed before the first install

Months 3–9

Prove the lift

  • Pilots run to completion with attribution instrumented from day one
  • The number published, with its method and its error bars
  • 25+ locations live; first mid-market chain converted to annual

Months 9–18

Prove the motion

  • 150–400 locations; $300–600k ARR
  • Second data source beyond Shopify; hospitality or field-service design partner
  • Seed raised on evidence, not narrative
