Cue — how the system is put together

Clienteling for a shop floor: a guest opts in, and the associate helping them sees who they are and what they want on the inside of their glasses. Everything below is read off the code at 072b7bb, not off a roadmap — where something is partial or stubbed, it is labelled.

Two sentences orient the whole design, and most review questions resolve to one of them.

The service key never leaves our services. Nothing in a browser, on a phone, or inside a released glasses package has ever held it.
The customer record never enters ours. Cue stores a pointer into the retailer's CRM — never a name, an address or an order. What is on the glass is fetched for that moment and not kept.

Credential sealing

A merchant's Shopify Admin token can read every customer, order and price in their store. It is sealed AES-256-GCM in the application, so the database only ever holds ciphertext, and the AAD binds it to the tenant — moving a sealed row between tenants fails to open rather than silently pointing Cue at the wrong shop. It is write-only: no API surface and no cuesea employee can read a token back, only a fingerprint and a key id. Rotation is supported (CUE_CRED_KEY_OLD), and every row records which key sealed it.

3 · Multi-tenancy, as invariants rather than intentions

Two of these were disclosures found in one day, in different layers, and they were the same mistake: state that is correct with one customer and wrong with two.

  • A socket's tenant is pinned at register and never re-read from a later payload. A client that wants to change retailer must reconnect.
  • Socket rooms, rosters, radio logs, leaderboards and stats are keyed by tenant. There is no global room.
  • Every analytics query is scoped by the signed-in user's tenant; only cue_admin may name another, and nobody can grant a role above their own.
  • The CRM adapter is resolved per tenant from a sealed credential — not from a process-wide client.
  • The guest roster endpoint is off for non-demo tenants: a list of every customer in a store is the exact shape tap-to-reveal exists to eliminate.

npm run test:tenants is ten negative assertions — it scores 3/10 against the commit before the fix and 10/10 after, which is the only way to know a leak test is worth anything.

4 · What is actually stored, and for how long

TableWhat it isRetention
tenants, users, devicesRetailers, staff accounts, glasses by serial.Kept. Not customer data.
engagementsOne guest interaction: zone, timings, outcome, sale, plus the CRM pointer and the three lines actually shown.Pointer, cue lines and recommendations redacted on the tenant's window. The shift survives.

guest_requestsWhat a guest asked to be brought: sku, size, need, note.The guest's own words and the pointer go; the sku, size and wait stay — that is a question about a shop, not a shopper.

tenant_crm_credentialsSealed merchant tokens.Kept until replaced. Never readable back.
retention_runsA receipt for every sweep.Kept.
Retention is redaction, not deletion. Deleting rows would destroy the analytics the product exists to produce — and a retailer who turned a 30-day window on and lost their quarter would turn it off and never turn it back on. So the sweep nulls what says something about a person and keeps what says something about a shift.

5 · Two paths end to end

6 · What is built, what is partial, what is not

Live

Works on hardware and in production

The lens and its card stack, voice, floor comms, presence and the front doors, guest requests, the check-in page, per-merchant sealed credentials, people auth with invites and resets, enforced retention, attribution, analytics, Studio and Console.

Partial

Works, with a caveat worth stating

Per-size stock now reads from Shopify variants but has not met a live store with real variants. Floor location is hardcoded to "Floor" pending a product metafield. Outbound email is unconfigured in production and reports as such. Floor comms has never been used by two people on a real floor, which is the only test of its phrase vocabulary.

Not built

Named rather than implied

Even Hub store listing. Anthropic, OpenAI and Google model providers are registered and stubbed — they raise rather than pretend. Gap's own /here route and app association. No Console panel for plates or for what is waiting on the floor.

7 · Failure modes and blast radius

If this failsWhat a guest seesWhat an associate sees
Model providerNothing — not their path."Model unavailable." Never a guess.
Speech providerNothing.Voice fails; the card, the cue and floor comms are unaffected.
Retailer CRMCheck-in still succeeds and their request still lands.No guest card; the request is still actionable, because it names a place rather than a person.
Depth (AI service)Check-in fails with a plain sentence and a suggestion to ask an associate.No new guests; the glasses stay up.
Analytics writeNothing.Nothing. Ingest is fire-and-forget by design — a reporting outage must never be felt mid-conversation.
A leaked plate tokenNothing until it is revoked.One row revoked, one plate reprinted, ~$11. The other door on the same plate keeps working.
PostgresCheck-in fails.Live sessions continue; nothing new starts. State is in-memory per tenant (Redis at scale).

9 · What I would put on a review agenda

  • A printed code is a bearer credential. Tokens made the URL opaque and revocable; they did not make it unshareable, and nothing printed can be. The tap door can be closed properly with rotating-cryptogram tags (the counter check is built and tested; the CMAC path has never met a physical tag). That is a purchasing decision, not an engineering one.
  • Single region, single replica, in-memory realtime state. Fine for a pilot; the Redis swap is designed for and not done.
  • CUE_CRED_KEY exists in one place — Railway. There are zero sealed rows today, so the cost of backing it up is nothing and the cost of losing it later is every merchant re-entering a token.
  • Two API keys were once pasted in plaintext and should be rotated.
  • The enterprise adapter is the real Gap dependency. The seam exists and is three methods wide; nothing above it knows which backend a tenant is on.
  • Naming debt: the repo, Railway project and environment variables still say gapvision while the product is Cue.
